Frequently asked questions

What the mod changes, why the base game behaves the way it does, and what to expect.

The basics

What does Hungry Pathing do?

During working hours it gives employed beavers a three-hour buffer on hunger and thirst, has them leave far jobs early enough to reach food before the penalty, tops them off before a shift they could not finish when food is near, and sends them to the closest stocked storage for the need at hand. Off duty the game's own behavior runs unchanged.

Why do beavers walk so far to eat in the base game?

Two reasons. A working beaver only leaves the job once the need is already at zero. And when it does, the game scores each food group by how many need points it would restore and only then picks the closest storage of the winning group. Sixteen of the seventeen foods carry a second "variety" need that is almost never full, so plain berries next to the site lose to bread at the district center nearly every time. Which food wins changes as variety needs fill up, so the long walks look random.

Do beavers eat more with this mod?

No. A unit of food restores a fixed 0.3 hunger and the game refuses to eat when a full unit would not fit, so nothing is wasted by eating earlier. Consumption per day equals the decay rate either way; only the timing moves. The mod's own checks simulate this over 300 days.

Does it handle water too?

Yes. Thirst is treated exactly like hunger, with its own buffer, and water is the only good that satisfies it, so those trips already go to the nearest water in the base game. The mod's gain for thirst is leaving early enough to avoid the movement penalty and topping off before a shift.

What happens in the evening and on days off?

Nothing new. The planner only runs for employed beavers during working hours. Evenings still use the game's own picking, which is where beavers chase variety and wellbeing buildings.

The buffer and the settings

What is the three-hour buffer, and where does the number come from?

Every need blueprint has an HoursWarningThreshold field. The base game sets it to 3.5 and 4.5 hours for bots' Energy and Biofuel and reads it for them, but ships every beaver need at zero and never reads it for beavers. The mod sets Hunger and Thirst to 3 hours through two partial blueprints and honors the value. Three hours at 0.8 hunger a day is 0.1 of the bar: the beaver starts walking with that in hand and arrives with it.

Can I change the buffer?

Yes, two ways. WarningHours in HungryPathing.cfg overrides it for every tracked need, or another data mod can set the blueprint field to a different value. Larger buffers mean beavers eat with more of the bar left, which is more walking during the shift with no penalty benefit unless storages are very far apart.

Can I try one rule at a time?

Yes. JustInTime, PreFuel, BuilderJobCheck, WorkTimeClosestFood and RedirectCriticalTrips each switch one rule. The daily log line says how often each fired, so you can see what a rule does for your colony. See CONFIGURATION.md for all keys.

Can I add other needs to the list?

The Needs key takes any need that is satisfied by a consumable good in a storage. Hunger and Thirst are the ones that matter for work; the variety needs are better left to the evening.

Compatibility

Does it work in co-op with BeaverBuddies?

It is built for it. Every decision reads only the simulation and the game's own path queries, with fixed ordering and tie-breaks, and nothing depends on the clock, the frame rate or random numbers. The condition is that every player runs the same version with an identical settings file; the log prints one Simulation settings: line to compare. The first live run was a BeaverBuddies session, and the host's replay of the same three days produced identical counters and consistency hashes with and without the guest.

Is it safe to add to or remove from an existing colony?

Yes. The mod stores nothing in saves; both of its components keep only caches rebuilt from the simulation. Even a save made while a beaver is on a trip the mod started records that trip as the game's own storage behavior.

Does it conflict with other mods?

It requires Harmony. A mod that also edits the Hunger or Thirst blueprint merges with this one in load order, and the buffer can always be forced with WarningHours. A mod that replaces the game's need-picking behavior for beavers would overlap with this one. Mods that change storages, goods or districts are fine: the planner reads the same registrations the game does.

Folktails and Iron Teeth?

Folktails tested; Iron Teeth expected to work, untested. Hunger and Thirst are shared beaver needs and no rule looks at the faction, but every run so far has been a Folktails colony. The part to watch is tubeways: they carry beavers much faster than walking, while the planner picks which storages are worth a path query by straight-line distance, so in a colony built around tubeways it can overlook the storage a tube reaches soonest. At worst a rule then does not fire, or sends a beaver to a storage that is closer on foot. Bots are untouched: they have their own picker and already use the warning field.

Does it change pathfinding?

No. Nothing in the navigation code is touched. The mod asks the game's own walker how long a walk would take and uses the answer to decide when to go and where.

Status

Why is it called a beta?

It builds against 1.1.2.4 with no warnings, every hook resolves against the game's assemblies, and the planner arithmetic passes its checks. It has run 36 in-game days in one 355-beaver colony, nearly all as a BeaverBuddies host with a guest, with no errors, identical results when days were replayed, and the before-and-after numbers on the overview. One colony is still one colony, and the builder rule has not yet seen a construction-heavy day, so it is a beta rather than a 1.0. If anything in it throws, it logs the stack trace once and switches itself off for the session.

How can I help?

Run it in a colony that does a lot of building, so the builder job check gets exercised, and post the daily [HungryPathing] lines from the log on the issue tracker together with anything that looked wrong. A save from an hour before the end of the shift, with and without the mod, is what the before-and-after numbers were made from.
